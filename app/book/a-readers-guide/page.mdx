# A Reader’s Guide

Before diving into the Notes and Reflections, a few words about how this book is structured—and how to approach it for the deepest understanding.

## What This Book Is

This is a window into how the Dhamma was communicated, person to person, from teacher to disciple. At its heart are the teachings of Venerable Ñāṇavimala captured in the handwritten notes of his student, Venerable Bambalapitiye Gnanaloka Thera.

For general readers, this book offers a collection of reflections—stories of a disciple's training that show what dedicated practice looks like, inspiring accounts of the Dhamma embodied in daily life, and the gradual transformation of a student under his teacher's guidance. These narratives provide an accessible entry into the teachings, illuminating the challenges faced, the wisdom offered, and the lived reality of walking the path.

For practitioners, behind these stories lie the original handwritten notes—a seven-year training curriculum rooted directly in the Buddha's *Suttas*. Preserved exactly as recorded, unfiltered, with minimal edits only for clarity, they offer complete guidance from ethical foundations through advanced practice.

## The Offering

For two and a half decades, these notes remained private—a way of remembering the Dhamma as it was lived. They now offer:

1. **A rare lineage preserved**—Ven. Ñāṇavimala combined the *Sutta* mastery of a Pāli scholar with the iron discipline of a forest ascetic. His approach—rooted directly in the Buddha's words—represents an almost-lost tradition.
2. **A complete training path**—from layman's ethical conduct to advanced monastic practice, these notes trace a gradual, systematic development rarely documented in such detail.
3. **In the spirit of *sandiṭṭhiko* (“visible here and now”) and *ehipassiko* (“come and see for yourself”)**, this book serves as encouragement to undertake your own meditation on the *Suttas*, which remain the ultimate authority.
4. **A glimpse of what is possible**—Ven. Ñāṇavimala's profound austerity exemplifies the Dhamma's highest capacity—not as a requirement for all, but as a measure of what becomes possible when renunciation is perfected.

## The Structure

Each chapter contains two parts: Notes and Reflections.

### Notes

Ven. Gnanaloka’s original records from 1994–2001. The discourses themselves were brief, often lasting only fifteen to thirty minutes, but carried weight that lingered long after. After each meeting, during retreats stretching weeks or months, he would write down what he could recall in his own words. Some had to be recopied, as the originals, battered by sun, rain, and long journeys, began to disintegrate.

These notes capture the teachings in concise language—often brief and direct, even abrupt—as they were written in the language of a personal encounter.

These are the core instructions—treat them as direct curriculum.

Edits were made only to correct grammar and sentence structure for readability. In a few instances of ambiguity, minor word adjustments were made to convey the intended meaning, always after direct consultation with Ven. Gnanaloka. All *Sutta* references, bracketed editor's notes [like this], and footnotes have been added for clarity.

Footnotes provide key context, clarifications, and additional details from Ven. Gnanaloka's spoken recollections.

Wherever the original notes contained words or phrases in ALL CAPS, underlining, or an asterisk (*), we have rendered them in **bold** in this edition to preserve the writer’s emphasis—signalling points regarded as especially significant or relevant.

### Reflections

The Reflections are crafted around a theme, not a chronological telling—though some chronological elements emerge naturally. Each explores a core aspect of the spiritual path: going directly to the Buddha's words as living practice, finding the path as a layman, facing sensuality honestly, development of compassion and when *mettā* becomes a shield, understandings of consciousness, radical equanimity as the body ages and fails, and walking fearlessly with only Dhamma as protection.

Rather than presenting a linear account of training, they offer contemplative meditations on essential teachings, revealed through scenes of a disciple receiving his master's guidance, interwoven with the life stories of both.

Written by practitioners who trained under Ven. Gnanaloka and studied his teachings, these reflections were developed from Ven. Gnanaloka's public talks, retreat teachings and interviews as well as other practitioners’ accounts. These Reflections provide context for the Notes.

Each stands as a contemplative piece and can be read on its own; however, reading them in sequence is recommended.

## The Teaching Progression

Read in sequence, they reveal the gradual unfolding of a disciple’s training under a noble master. The record is not continuous. Some notes were never written down. Natural gaps arose when Shanaka was away—studying at university in Cyprus, working in Colombo, on solitary retreats, or during long periods in India and the Himalayas. What remains, however, carries the full weight of the teaching and traces the outline of a training both personal and timeless.

## How to Read

The book alternates between Notes (presented chronologically as they were recorded) and Reflections throughout.

We recommend reading in sequence—following the same journey Shanaka (Ven. Gnanaloka) took as his understanding deepened over seven years. The Reflections build upon each other, gradually providing context. The Notes can be revisited as reference material; their meaning deepens with repeated reading.

However, two distinct reader groups may benefit from different emphasis:

### For Experienced Practitioners

Read the Notes as direct curriculum and use Reflections for context and inspiration. Treat this as a training manual. Test teachings in your own practice. Return often—let understanding deepen over time.

### For Curious New Seekers

Start with the Reflections—read them as inspiring stories and a gentle introduction to the path. They offer glimpses into what dedicated practice looks like. When ready for deeper practice, turn to the Notes for direct exploration of the teachings.

### For Everyone

- **Read slowly.** Don't rush. Let teachings settle within.
- **Read contemplatively.** This is not an academic text to analyze but a source for quiet reflection.
- **Return often.** Meaning unfolds over time and through practice.
- **Follow the *Sutta* references.** The notes constantly point to the Buddha's words—these are the living sources from which all authentic Dhamma flows.

## On Translation and Language

The Reflections were developed from sources in both English and Sinhala. Some quoted remarks have been translated from Sinhala, combined from multiple instances for clarity, or slightly adapted for readability. The original meaning and spirit have been carefully preserved.

This book aimed to address the challenge of being both authentic to the source and accessible to a broad audience. At times, this meant expressing certain Pāli terms and subtle scriptural meanings in simpler language. While some nuanced details may not be immediately apparent to readers unfamiliar with the original terminology, this approach was chosen to bridge the gap for newcomers while preserving the essence of the teaching.

**On Pāli terms:** You'll encounter many—the language of early Buddhist scriptures. Most are explained in context. A comprehensive Glossary at the end provides reference for all terms.

## Using the Appendix

The Appendix is organized for different levels of practice and commitment:

- **For Lay Practitioners:** Curated teachings on ethical conduct, generosity, and meditation suitable for household life.
- **For Monastics:** Comprehensive curriculum addressing training for those who have gone forth.
- **Walking Contemplations:** The precise sequence Ven. Ñāṇavimala would verbalize during *caṅkama* (walking meditation)—rare insight into a master's moment-to-moment practice.
- **The Source of Wisdom: Canonical References:** All *Suttas* mentioned throughout the book for deeper reading and reflections.

## This Book as Gateway

View this as a guide pointing to the ultimate source: the Buddha's own words. Keep these texts close while reading:

- *The Connected Discourses of the Buddha* (Saṁyutta Nikāya)
- *The Middle Length Discourses of the Buddha* (Majjhima Nikāya)
- *The Numerical Discourses of the Buddha* (Aṅguttara Nikāya)
- *The Word of the Buddha* by Ven. Nyanatiloka Mahāthera
