Chapter 06

Restrain, Abstain and Endure
(Dec 1995 – Feb 1996)

3 December 1995

x All these blessings and merit that you have accrued—may you achieve your goal.

x You may practice as mentioned in Sutta 107, 117, 125 of Majjhima [Gaṇakamoggallāna Sutta (MN107; to Gaṇaka Moggallāna); Mahācattārīsaka Sutta (MN117; the great forty); Dantabhūmi Sutta (MN125; the grade of the tamed)] and Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda Sutta (MN61; advice to Rāhula at Ambalaṭṭhikā), if you are keen to enter the path, then Khandhasaṁyutta (SN22; connected discourses on the aggregates) and Saḷāyatanasaṁyutta (SN35; connected discourses on the six sense bases). This effort you make in this direction will be the highest form of merit. And will be the only way to repay parents, relatives and ultimately to all beings. It will be a blessing.

x As a layman, Suttas mentioned to Mahānāma.3

x Know when you [return], you would have to accommodate a bit. You may not find people with an understanding for this. In that case, you would not try to convince them or convert them, but with [a] heart of mettā, you should also keep away from them and should not follow their inclinations.

x You will truly be able to help your relatives and everyone else only if you purify yourself. Because from a purified mind, purified actions follow.

x Bhava Sutta (SN45.164; existence)

x “Iti kho, bhikkhave, dhammā dhamme abhisandenti, dhammā dhamme paripūrenti apārā pāraṁ gamanāyā’ti.”

[“And so, mendicants, good qualities flow on and fill up from one to the other, for going from the near shore to the far shore.”]

4 December 1995

x [Quote from Ven. Ñāṇavimala] “Regarding Saddhātissa, it may be true that we would have been together. But one must stick to the present. As the present makes the future. One must control the mind. They are just thoughts (past and future). This name seems to be very familiar, we would have been together in past lives. But you must stick to the All Enlightened One’s words and advice. As in Mahāparinibbāna Sutta (DN16; the great passing, the Buddha’s last days), where the Buddha said, ‘After my passing away, that your teacher will be the Dhamma and the Vinaya’.” 4

x [Quote from Ven. Ñāṇavimala] “I also got something after 20 years in the Saṅgha. There is kamma behind [this].” [Personal Note:] (Maybe an attainment etc.)5

x Ariya sīla (noble virtue) is observed when one is practicing Satipaṭṭhāna (foundation of mindfulness).

x You can’t fall into negligence because you have a lot to work on. That is to overcome the hindrances (five of them) and to attain jhāna. And that state of peace and so as to see its true nature of anicca, dukkha and anatta, because it is also a state of consciousness.

x Mātugāma Sutta (SN37.1; agreeable and disagreeable, first).6

x Sutta 39 and 40 in Majjhima leading to jhāna—Mahā-Assapura Sutta (MN39; the greater discourse at Assapura); Cūḷa-Assapura Sutta (MN40; the shorter discourse at Assapura).

x A Gradual Training in 107 and 125 in Majjhima—Gaṇakamoggallāna Sutta (MN107; to Gaṇaka Moggallāna); Dantabhūmi Sutta (MN125; the grade of the tamed).

x You must not try to preach and so you must center on yourself. Based on your pure state of mind, purified action will flow. To benefit both you and all beings.

x You cannot be negligent as it is so uncertain. The sāsana (dispensation/teaching) nearly disappeared twice in Sri Lanka. It can disappear again. So we do not know what will happen tomorrow. So make haste right now.

x To get saṁvega (think of the endless past, what we had to go through), e.g. the tears more than the four oceans etc. and what is ahead of us in Dīgha 26—Cakkavatti-Sīhanāda Sutta (DN26; the lion’s roar on the turning of the wheel).

x Dīgha 27—Aggañña Sutta (DN27; on knowledge of beginnings).

5 December 1995

x Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda Sutta (MN61; advice to Rāhula at Ambalaṭṭhikā): You do things and follow actions that benefit both parties or that which reduces lobha (greed), dosa (hate) and moha (delusion).

x The way you reflected in the past [how] the present things are formed; for example, you have acted with ‘I’ conceit etc. Therefore, now [there occurs] the present manifestation of the body and so on. As time goes on you will understand it deeply. Nothing from outside could help you to see things in reality. But the mind [itself] should be slowly established and trained to reflect [on things] as not mine and so on.

x You must make plans for the near future (e.g. going to the station in the morning) and quickly come back to the
present and must practice sati sampajañña (mindfulness and clear comprehension). This should be the way throughout.

x Past inclination would manifest, but one must delight in overcoming those inclinations and coming to mastery of them, in the present life. It is a great difficulty to overcome attractions to people [with whom] one was connected to in past lives.7

x Whatever momentary happiness there is, it is a temporary intoxication. But far more suffering is connected with it. Therefore, overcome it by developing the opposite faculties [5 indriyas—5 spiritual faculties] and [take] delight [in them]. For example, Ven. Nanda when he was an Arahant, a devī [female celestial being] from the deva loka [celestial realm] who had once been his consort appeared. However, he replied that, as an Arahant, he had no such connections.8

x Majjhima Sutta 131—Bhaddekaratta Sutta (MN131; a single excellent night); practice like that. Stick to the present.

x When you go to certain temples or meditation centers (Remember Mātugāma)—Mātugāma Saṁyutta (SN37; connected discourses on women).

x Try to avoid such contact [with the opposite sex] as far as possible.

• Don’t look at them.

• If you have to look at them, don’t talk.

• If you have to talk, then think of the asubha.9

x When you have attained the first jhāna, you should go to the second and so on until one reaches Nibbāna. Never should one stick to even good states. However, as a Bodhisatta, the Buddha was never satisfied with good states.

x Certain verses or Suttas that had an impact, isolate them and repeat it over and over again.

x Further qualities to be developed when one is established in five [spiritual] faculties (indriya): saddhā (faith/confidence), viriya (energy/effort), sati (mindfulness), samādhi (concentration), paññā (wisdom)—[Paṭhamavibhaṅga Sutta (SN48.9; analysis (1))].

x There are six qualities to be grown—Mahānāma Sutta (AN6.10; Mahānāma)

• Recollect the Tathāgata—Happiness should develop in connection to repeated recollection.

• Recollect the Dhamma

• Recollect the Saṅgha

• You should recollect your own virtues

• You should recollect your own generosity (cāga)

• You should recollect the devas

6 December 1995

x Daily practice of a bhikkhu is to overcome the five hindrances and to develop the five indriya.10

x And we come back to Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda Sutta (MN61; advice to Rāhula at Ambalaṭṭhikā) (tīṇi sucaritāni—three ways of good conduct) ariyakanta sīla,11 this is accomplished when you practice Satipaṭṭhāna—Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta (DN22; the greater discourse on the foundations of mindfulness).

x You should act in the way of Mahāsuññata Sutta (MN122; the greater discourse on voidness). That is verbal and mental actions.

x Majjhima 131—Bhaddekaratta Sutta (MN131; a single excellent night) is very important, because it is not worth following the past (past lives etc.) and having future hopes and definite plans etc.

x Visions and dreams and so on, one makes a note of it but does not follow them. Some may be right, some maybe not. But should always and totally rely on the words of the Buddha.

x And regarding attainment; how can anyone know unless declared by the Buddha or an Arahant with abhiññā? It was forbidden for bhikkhus to declare their attainment, how much so laymen. And what’s the point of speculating? What one should ensure is that one is acting in the present in accordance with a way that brings one closer to a state of peace and certainty regarding the Dhamma.

x [Quote from Ven. Ñāṇavimala] “For example, there was [a yogi] who thought [they were] an Anāgāmi, and I said that [they] will go mad if [they think] like that. And after one year or so, it happened that [they] went mad.”12

x In Majjhima 105—Sunakkhatta Sutta (MN105; to Sunakkhatta), how many came and declared in front of the Buddha their final attainment. Among them some were true and some were not.

x It is peaceful when one practices the Dhamma [in the present moment].

x Now when you [return] to the layman’s life, it is as if you are going on piṇḍapāta: you are going to give [Dhamma] and this is the highest giving. First you may establish them in dāna, sīla, bhāvanā—Sigālaka Sutta (DN31; to Sigālaka). Furthermore you can [guide them] to establish them in Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha. For those who are further interested in attaining the path: [the] Suttas taught to Mahānāma and Anāthapiṇḍika and Rāhulovāda Sutta [Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda Sutta (MN61; advice to Rāhula at Ambalaṭṭhikā)], mettā bhāvanā (as mentioned by Ven. Ñāṇamoli).

x If you are unable to practise the three ways of conduct perfectly, do not have remorse; what is past is past. However, must make a determination to avoid it in the future.

x You must encourage others in paviveka (solitude)—for laypeople, at least on the poya days. As for yourself, try to retire [into solitude] as [much] as possible, 2 or 3 days before and after the poya.

x The way you act and direct the mind in that environment is very creative and constructive. One must constantly concentrate on one’s practice and only by one’s example that one encourages others.

x Attaining the jhānas, step by step training, the Suttas 107 and 125 of Majjhima—Gaṇakamoggallāna Sutta (MN107; to Gaṇaka Moggallāna); Dantabhūmi Sutta (MN125; the grade of the tamed) and then to attain to the path, Khandhasaṁyutta (SN22; connected discourses on the aggregates) and Saḷāyatanasaṁyutta (SN35; connected discourses on the six sense bases).

x In any case, daily one must make it a point even if it is in the night for this bhāvanā (meditation).13

x One must not overload with intellectual grasping of the Suttas. One picks out one or two and brings the mind to that actual nature of function mentioned in the Suttas.14 This is very important!

x To get saṁvega (spiritual urgency): Dīgha 26—Cakkavatti-Sīhanāda Sutta (DN26; the lion’s roar on the turning of the wheel); Anamataggasaṁyutta (SN15; connected discourses on without discoverable beginning).

x And never be satisfied with good states.

x You must establish others in only what you yourself are established in and what you clearly see and have experi-
enced.

x When some people (laypeople) recite the gāthās etc. in their shrine room, certain devatās (blood relatives) come and impart certain information. They have a wider field of knowledge. e.g. when Reverend Sir went on cārikā in Minneriya how he was offered a lunch pack.15

x All the merit that you have accrued and the past background in the Dhamma etc. will come to fruit, even though you have to deviate a bit from suitable conditions for the practice presently.

x Do you remember Makhādeva Sutta (MN83; king Makhādeva) in Majjhima, the noble practice [of renunciation] started by Makhādeva which led to the Brahma world, [being] stopped [after] king Nimi. The Buddha warns how not to be the last in the Ariyan lineage [noble lineage].16

7 December 1995

x When you use all that is available then it spoils the Saṅgha That is what has happened in the temple.17

x What you should do is restrain or abstain and endure.

x This is the Way.

x The bhikkhu’s attitude is to leave things.

x When he endures, he does not feed on craving.

x To like solitude, it is necessary to have kamma in the past. Or it should be cultivated. Because it is necessary to strive in solitude to experience certain things that cannot be reasoned out, like jhānas and vipassanā knowledge.

x Sutta 9 in Majjhima—Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta (MN9; right view)

x Like Raṭṭhapāla and others, like the one who was married, [where] the wife kept the child in front of him—because

he was an arahant, there [was] no effect.18 It is acintiya [inconceivable] at thought level, to [conceive of] how arahants think. (They are looking at the whole process from outside). As they are considering [it] as ‘not mine’ and
‘not I.' That’s why most of the arahants are silent.

x You must consider yours’ and others’ welfare. You have already done it with your relatives. It’s the beginning.

x But never forget your welfare which is most important. And it is the first priority! “Attadatthaṁ paratthena” [Let one not neglect one's own welfare for the sake of another's] (Attadatthatthera Vatthu, Dhp 166).

8 December 1995

x If you follow another religion, that means you never got a true understanding in the Dhamma.

Important:

x Some may decide to be anāgārika and so on.19 But one must consider this may create asmi-māna [egocentric conceit], that I am better than others or different to others etc. It is important out of respect and gratitude to the Buddha that one accommodates, and has the Buddha as one’s constant aim. For three years I was an upāsaka and I was quite
alright like that, but I reflected how I got all that
opportunity, it is because of the Saṅgha. It is always good not to separate oneself from the outside framework. Always respecting the Buddha, Dhamma and Saṅgha.

x By entering the Saṅgha, one encourages the Saṅgha and brings unity.

x The less you need to support yourself as a bhikkhu or as a layman, the better it is.

x Always, [you] must not worry [about] what changes from outside. But [you] must know even [with] things that seem to be pleasant, [that they] are connected with feelings. Anyone who delights in feelings, delights in suffering. So, one must be happy that one is constantly following the way that learns to delight in the Dhamma, that is ultimately developed beyond feelings.

x Always developing equanimity, see even that the highest state of mind is connected with consciousness. For consciousness needs an object and depends on the body, so it is anicca (impermanent), dukkha (suffering), and anatta (not-self).

x To do all these it takes some time. So, you have to see it as a layman or bhikkhu that one first reduces saṅkhāras that is coming up till jhāna. So, it is a gradual process of reducing saṅkhāras and overcoming them.

x Whenever you notice that you are delighting in some kind of pleasant feeling, [know that] there is delusion at that time. From time to time the attraction to the opposite sex will come. You should know that for many lives we have given into this strongest fetter. It is not easy to overcome overnight, only by asubha that one sees the delusion behind it. And one overcomes it immediately. And again Rāhulovāda Sutta [Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda Sutta (MN61; advice to Rāhula at Ambalaṭṭhikā)], for my welfare and for others, [one] should firmly stick to [the practice of] learning to reflect before, while and after all actions.

x When meeting people from other religions, you should instruct them (mentioned by Venerable Piyadassi) and encourage them to follow their religion.20 All religions talk about dāna (giving), sīla (virtue) and bhāvanā (meditation/ mental development). But in this Dhamma there is this Satipaṭṭhāna (foundation of mindfulness).

x All religions and their founders are ones who reached a certain high state. But it lasts only for some time and collapses again. Whether it is Islam, Hinduism, Christianity, Judaism.

x The western countries are materially driven, each one wanting to advance more than the other. Then war begins. And then when everything collapses, they go to the churches etc. But when things become alright, again they get back to building. This is because their spiritual experience is based on micchā diṭṭhi (wrong view).

x When you [wonder], how [does] the arahant think, it is not possible to understand. But if we just consider; he experiences things from a distance.

x There is happiness in worldly life and suffering of the renounced life. But the suffering of the renounced life is better than the happiness of the worldly life. When I went on cārikā, I was completely independent; don't think it was easy. There was suffering and it is like that until death. No formation is complete or satisfactory. And all that we experience [is], that which we have done or created. So making sure the mind is always directed to a state (where one will be free from all this suffering forever) without creating disharmony or conflict with the surrounding that one is in.

x You must not try to be a scholar, and going on preaching etc. like in the temples, one activity after the other—more saṅkhāras (volitional formations). Satisfying the dāyakas [lay devotees] etc. and losing sight of the essential practice, which in return makes the Saṅgha deteriorate.

x The Suttas: Majjhima 131, 19, 20, 107, 117, 125—Bhaddekaratta Sutta (MN131; a single excellent night); Dvedhāvitakka Sutta (MN19; two kinds of thought); Vitakkasaṇṭhāna Sutta (MN20; the removal of distracting thoughts); Gaṇakamoggallāna Sutta (MN107; to Gaṇaka Moggallāna); Mahācattārīsaka Sutta (MN117; the great forty); Dantabhūmi Sutta (MN125; the grade of the tamed).

x When you get back [home], Mahānāma Suttas.21

x This way of activity (mentioned in Mahānāma Suttas) gives a chance to glimpse the ultimate at that level, as a layman.

9 December 1995

x When you direct your mind to a certain sphere, then as if by force, you get support and response in that direction. For example: when someone is trying to climb a mountain, he begins to meet people who are on the same path and they help each other to come to the goal.

x One must learn in all one’s activities to stick to the present. Majjhima 131—Bhaddekaratta Sutta (MN131; a single excellent night).

x One must not try to think whether one has attained the path or one will attain the path. But must stick to the practice as advised by the Buddha, not sticking to a person, etc.

x Practice according to the Suttas 107, 117, 125
Gaṇakamoggallāna Sutta (MN107; to Gaṇaka Moggallāna); Mahācattārīsaka Sutta (MN117; the great forty);
Dantabhūmi Sutta (MN125; the grade of the tamed) leading to the jhāna. And if one wants to enter the path: Khandha and Saḷāyatana Saṁyuttas. This is almost the noble eightfold path.

x Seeing always the causes of this suffering and removing it. It is removing the nīvaraṇas (hindrances).

x There is always the danger that one may become a scholar. In the beginning, the first arahants heard only one Sutta. You may make note of certain Suttas that had an impact. And may repeat them. Only as the practice progresses that you may refer to more Suttas.

x You must try your best to help others by your example. When you turn away. And when they see that, it is an encouragement for them to do the same.

x When you don’t think of a person or an individual, but as a whole, for example as the Mahā Saṅgha, that is spiritual. One must regularly develop the delight connected to Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha.

x Purāṇa kamma (Old action/Past kamma) is the present body and what is experienced with it. And making sure in the present one reflects wisely the presently arisen phenomenon, so that future arising would not take place.

x May you be successful in all your endeavors (last words).

11 December 1995

x From sati (mindfulness), it is possible to attain jhāna (meditative absorption).

x As you go on, one day you should determine to learn the Dhamma in the exact words of the Buddha, that is in Pāli. There is no other language to [better] describe the ideas expressed by the Buddha, except Pāli. The other thing is when the translator translates with his defiled mind the meaning undoubtedly changes.

x You may read the Thera Gāthās (Thag; verses of the elder monks) to get inspiration. Like this you can add some knowledge that fits to the main practice.

x What is most important now is to establish these practices so that they become a habit. In all conditions.

x As soon as you concentrate and see every experience from the point of view of the Buddha’s teaching, all difficulties and suffering vanish.

x Then one looks at it from a distance, as not mine etc. [The practice] goes up to the end (arahantship), until one is continuously experiencing from that point of view. You see they are just processes and phenomena. When you stick to this awareness certainty should come and doubt should vanish.

x May you be a help for many beings. It starts at home. All who have a body will go through, some time or the other, the suffering connected to it. May they all come to the utter release from this suffering. May you go ahead of all of them, that will be a great help.

x Most of life is created by sex. Most of the time we meet people who were connected to us from the past. One is totally blinded [by] illusion when one is sexually attracted. And [once] involved, [mental] bondages are made. Only after all that is done, one sees the suffering connected [with it] and involved in it. So one must take care!

x In the Dhamma when help is possible to be given, you may do so but not at personal expense. Otherwise one may get on, making all these things fall off,22 because what we see, hear, etc. there is kamma behind it, but they are just feelings, perceptions: “not mine,” “not I.”

x You may discuss with your parents, relatives, telling them clearly and definitely your intentions.

x Later on, when you are definitely certain you may act like Raṭṭhapāla—Raṭṭhapāla Sutta (MN82; on Raṭṭhapāla). But you should act in such a way that will create harmony, and not disharmony.23 And especially your relatives have been useful and helpful from a young age. And they are connected to you from past lives. So, you have to help each other.

12 December 1995

x Make sure you develop the mind now, not to worry about what is ahead and what has happened. But learning to observe the present with skill. Try to get established in this until you are here. Then you will be able to face anything
with an undisturbed mind. And when you try to avoid foreseen activities and bondages, restraining your inner inclination, remember this is kamma. And when you are fully directing the mind, things would change from outside.

x Very Important: You must learn to get along alone. Because what is ultimately to be cultivated, goes beyond outside access and intervention, and in any case the outside situations change too. I too had support for five years and was left alone. Then I have to go through the Suttas by myself. There is kamma behind all these things. You may meet certain people who would give support at different levels. You must be like a clever sailor or a mountaineer, not going straight through but criss-cross[ing] never losing the aim.

x For your uncle: Sigālaka Sutta (DN31; to Sigālaka), Mettā Bhāvanā, Buddhism in a Nutshell by Nārada Thera.

x You may discuss it with your parents and relatives. Prestige is not important. In any case all this material gain ultimately brings disaster. And you may tell your future aim. And you may openly tell the difficulties faced when attending the classes etc.

x Even to enter the Saṅgha, there is kamma behind it. So one must try to support the bhikkhus to get on undisturbed.

x It is impossible that you spend the whole time in pīti [rapture] and happiness.

x Regarding women, act in the way Venerable Ānanda was advised to do—Mahāparinibbāna Sutta (DN16; the great passing, the Buddha’s last days).24

x When you go to the classes and when you practice restraint etc. you are practicing the Dhamma.

13 December 1995

x We may meet again or not or these conditions may come or will not come. But what is important is that you direct to the Dhamma in whatever condition one is in. That this state of mind is undisturbed. One lives in the present, that itself is a happiness. And making the conditions more suitable to do that undisturbed and continuously in a harmonized way. Because when the objects change very fast, it is difficult to practice this.

x The Hindus and so on, when their teacher dies, the pupils [seek] out where he is [re-]born and bring him back to teach. The yogis, no doubt, work very hard. They consider that which they [strive] for as permanent, but it is [still] within consciousness and therefore a formation. All formations are anicca (impermanent), dukkha (suffering) and anatta (non-self). They have asmi-māna (ego-conceit), but the bhikkhus do not.

x Many arahants at the time of the Buddha were previously in the Brahmaloka25 or one who has cultivated jhāna. When they met the Buddha they easily attained jhāna and arahantship. In any case they experienced what they have done (the kamma) to come back to the human world (manussaloka).

x You may not be able to sit in bhāvanā26 all the time. But you may go a step below, and contribute towards the concentration by reading the Suttas, reciting striking stanzas and memorizing etc.

x The Buddha as Bodhisatta (king Makhādeva—Makhādeva Sutta (MN83; king Makhādeva)) arose many times in the Brahmaloka, but came back, still a puthujjana (common worldling). Until he understood the paṭiccasamuppāda (dependent origination). Then he became the Dhamma Rāja (king of Dhamma).
