Chapter 03

The Way You Deal With Your Parents
(Oct 1994 – Dec 1994)

26 October 1994

  • It is good to start the practice early in life (earlier the better).
  • You must see in your own self [the body] the impurities and how it is rushing towards old age.
  • When one sees this with full concentration, there is no craving (paṭiccasamuppāda). You must also see the other bodies with the same destiny and impure nature.
  • When you are old, it is difficult.
  • Watch over the three ways of conduct4 and practice mettā bhāvanā, and wait.
  • If you have to do a job [get one] that will give you enough time and is in accordance with Dhamma.5

28 October 1994

  • Suttas to Mahānāma. Though they were laymen they attained the path. Practice the way it is taught there. Buddhānussati, Dhammānussati and Saṅghānussati then mettā, anicca
    saññā.    6
  • As a layman or a bhikkhu, in any case, Majjhima 61— Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda Sutta (MN61; advice to Rāhula at Ambalaṭṭhikā).
  • Majjhima 107, 125 gradually to jhāna and the anicca of that—Gaṇakamoggallāna Sutta (MN107; to Gaṇaka Moggallāna), Dantabhūmi Sutta (MN125; the grade of the tamed).
  • Majjhima 64—Mahāmālunkya Sutta (MN64; the greater discourse to Mālunkyāputta).
  • Word of the Buddha [by Ven. Nyanatiloka Mahāthera] (not what is mentioned in the commentaries). But follow advice strictly from the early Buddhist texts.7
  • Mahā Parinibbāna Sutta (there is a lot of valuable advice in it)—Mahāparinibbāna Sutta (DN16; the great passing, the Buddha’s last days)
  • Word of the Buddha [by Venerable Nyanatiloka Mahāthera] has a very good right view (sammā-diṭṭhi) in essence. You can practice ānāpānasati but with Right View (sammā-diṭṭhi).
  • Soon as you enter the Saṅgha, there is certain training one has to do (new bhikkhus). Venerable Mettavihārī knows the Suttas and their grouping very well.
  • Now you are 24, you may have to start an apprenticeship which your father would also like. Then before you take up another job or anything, take a one year holiday. Then you may visit certain aranyas and stay for a couple of months and see the practice.
  • I recommend [several] aranyas: Galduwa, Wathuruwila, and also Kanduboda. They [all] have branch [monasteries]. They have branches.
  • But you must make sure you are given all opportunities to devote fully to the practice; otherwise, impossible to attain to the path.
  • If you decide to be a monk after that one year, make sure it is under those conditions. Otherwise, it is better to be a layman.
  • The Saṅgha may disappear any moment.
  • If you are a laymen try to join an organization like Red Cross or civil service where you can perform action doing merit at the same time supporting the body.
  • If you take up a job in the management field you may earn a lot of money and now you are 24 but 25, 26, 27, these ages you will be approached by parents and relatives to have a partner. Here you have to make a decision. [Personal Note:] I must be frank about this [subject of marriage].
  • Jotipāla and Ghaṭīkāra—Ghaṭīkāra Sutta (MN81; Ghaṭīkāra the potter). Ghaṭīkāra was a layman—an anāgāmī, who reached the Suddhāvāsa [Brahma world]. Jotipāla joined the Saṅgha, attained nothing at that birth and became an arahant as Sammāsambuddha [Gotama] before Ghaṭīkāra.
  • [Quote from Ven. Ñāṇavimala] So I don’t know, these are only suggestions. You might know a better way.8
  • At this stage, it is most important to do your studies properly.
  • I really don’t know your exact intentions, all this Dhamma knowledge etc. (The Reverend Sir seems to ask “for what”).9
  • All my blessings to your endeavors.

29 October 1994

  • How much work you have in the layman’s life, still you should allocate certain time for the practice and in any case during poya [holiday] you should take sīla. If possible before and after poya you may retire [for] 2 or 3 days at a stretch.
  • You need merit, don’t fear merit.
  • The Buddha was once asked why he was so powerful? etc. He replied how he did mettā bhāvanā and how he rose in the Brahma world and how he became the Cakkavatti king many times as a result of his puñña kamma.
  • Learn to experience everything from a distance, as everything is anicca, dukkha and anatta.

30 November 1994

  • Were you free from disturbance from inside and outside. From inside it is not possible to be free from stray thoughts. Unless you attain jhāna (meditative absorption) where you have one thought.
  • To attain jhāna, you should be highly sīlavanta (virtuous). The Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda Sutta (MN61; advice to Rāhula at Ambalaṭṭhikā); in this Sutta not only verbal and physical, but mental. So, you must purify the mind. It is based on the state of mind that one’s physical and verbal actions follow.
  • Therefore, the mind should be watched as in Sutta No. 19 in Majjhima Nikāya—Dvedhāvitakka Sutta (MN19; two kinds of thought), and controlled as mentioned in Sutta 20—Vitakkasaṇṭhāna Sutta (MN20; the removal of distracting thoughts).
  • You may read Khandhasaṁyutta (SN22; connected discourses on the aggregates) and Saḷāyatanasaṁyutta (SN35; connected discourses on the six sense bases). And note down striking passages in Pāli. Repeat it and go through them slowly.10
  • When you are a layman, you must make sure to follow what was advised to Mahānāma.11

x Samādhi (Stillness): mettā bhāvanā (cultivation of loving-kindness), Satipaṭṭhāna (foundation of mindfulness), Cāga (generosity and letting go): giving up (even the hindrances), Bahussuta (knowledgeable/learned): reading the Suttas daily, a bit.

x Mettā bhāvanā should be practiced as advised by Ñāṇamoli Thera [The Practice of Loving-Kindness].12

x You must get saṁvega (spiritual urgency) by reading what is mentioned in the Maharagama Pirith Book [The Mirror of the Dhamma compiled by Ven. Nārada and Ven. Kassapa].

x Caturarakkha bhāvanā [Four Protective Meditations].

  • If you want to be a bhikkhu, you must really look into your heart.

25 December 1994

  • Explanation of walking [meditation] of Rev. Ñāṇavimala, from the kuṭi to dāna sālā (sati sampajañña),13 it is looking at the body from outside as if looking at another person, especially the movement of the legs and seeing the impermanence in the movement of the legs constituted by flesh, bones, and in this way establishing dispassion and at other times sitting down to meditate and attain jhāna.14

x Working is a good idea, because of your concentration you may achieve good results in meditation [and at work]; and there may arise temptation when higher things are offered [at work] and at that time you must be careful.

  • Do not stick to one meditation object, but to eradicate different kinds of defilement you should choose [different meditation objects] and practice.
  • Verses in the Dhammapada (Dhp; verses of the Dhamma) and Word of the Buddha by Ven. Nyanatiloka Mahāthera are good.
  • Practice mettā bhāvanā, don’t just wish happiness, but do it in the way taught by Ven. Ñāṇamoli [The Practice of Loving-Kindness].
  • Don’t pray or wish that you should come to this or that, there should not be any tension.
  • You must meet the teacher and discuss when there is a disturbance of thought or such like.

26 December 1994

  • Consider the way you deal with your parents when dealing with other Godly beings. You must work and do your duty in such a way to get their blessing. But do not go [to them] for ultimate refuge, as they are still in saṁsāra (the cycle of suffering and rebirth). [You] must go for refuge in Dhamma.
  • We have created this situation.
  • Try to practice this awareness of the body (that is, as if looking at it from outside) or as if observing another body, cultivate it or practice it as much as possible because at that time you are living in the present.
  • Read, grasp and practice a couple of Suttas. Concentrate on them.
  • Don’t try to memorise a lot of Suttas, like in the case of a scholar. Practice is important.
  • It is very important, this practice (to reflect on all activities during the day and to think over these activities one by one before your sleep, then to make [a] determination [adhiṭṭhāna] to avoid any activities you did the previous day that are regrettable).

It is good to end the day with mettā bhāvanā, after doing Buddhānussati [recollecting the Buddha’s virtues] and when doing mettā bhāvanā in the end reflect on the
impermanence of that also.

  • [Your] Father too will definitely come to understand. He will definitely give his deep blessings.
  • When certain impressions come, when reading or reciting the gāthās [verses] or Suttas, don’t sort of feel, I should immediately go, get there and things like that.15 No frustration, wishes and so on. You must remember that situations change. So, you should work for it in the desired direction continuously and constantly. Like when you desire to reach a certain place (e.g., Kandy city), simply by wishing you would not get there. But if you start moving in the right direction and working towards it, stage by stage, you would finally come there. It is the same way.

27 December 1994

  • There should be a good foundation of sīla (virtue) and then jhāna (meditative absorption) and Satipaṭṭhāna (foundation of mindfulness).
  • First 10 Suttas in Majjhima Nikāya leading to final liberation.16
  • Purified mind is followed by purified action. It is important to first concentrate on the state of mind then after that other actions will be all wholesome (first Dhammapada verses).17
  • Read a couple of Dhammapada verses daily.
  • While attending [to] normal activities, it is not possible to attain the jhānas (meditative absorption). But when you practice sati or awareness, then when the time for sitting meditation, it is more supportive to attain the jhānas.
  • When you practice this awareness of the body (that is looking at it as if another body) and the things that constitute the impurities, then there is a turning away of the mind from the body (dispassion). Then due to this a certain feeling of peace may arise. Now one must understand the impermanence of feeling and should not accommodate any craving for it. When one does that, [then] clinging (upādāna), existence (bhava), birth (jāti), old age, sickness—all this is not there (paṭicca samuppāda).
  • But to do all this, one must build a sound foundation of sīla (virtue).
  • Don’t think about what to do in the future or how to act because all situations change. When the time comes, consider and decide.

28 December 1994

  • Must make sure there is a good basis for sīla (virtue), for the Dhamma building to be constructed.
  • One cannot always be in meditation or jhāna (meditative absorption). But when one is attending to other work like moving about, one should establish oneself in mindfulness as much as possible and it becomes the most wholesome condition to attain the jhānas when one sits down to it.
  • One should establish three ways of conduct and should reflect every evening before sleeping [in regards to] any activity that is regrettable—that which should be avoided the next day and so on. It should continue day after day. This practice should be put into effect without delay.
  • Then one practices Satipaṭṭhāna to overcome the hindrances, and then attains the jhānas. Afterwards one turns away from that as well, that is when one enters the path.
  • Know you have a theoretical grasp, but experience is important. You must experience it.
  • Whenever there is a decision to be made and you do not know what to do, then you should practice mettā bhāvanā before you sleep and then think about the ultimate goal you want to achieve, and the decision you have to make, and then you can fall asleep. In the morning you would get the answer.
  • Normally, reasoning out is not correct. This way it is more reliable.
  • In a nice appealing fashion, someone may appear (maybe the wife in past lives), you must know that this is Māra.18
  • But when you practice Satipaṭṭhāna (foundation of mindfulness) it is quite safe. Don’t worry about anything. Every moment should be [a] pleasant abiding.
  • You must be one with the Mahāsaṅgha, not considering an individual, then though we are separate in body, we will be one in mind.

At a Previous Discussion

  • You must go and stay in a jungle, if you are forced from inside, not otherwise. You must see a tremendous need to turn away from the kāma-loka [world of sensual pleasure].
  • A lot of arahants were near the Himalayan region. This area is extremely difficult. This was the reason that many became yogis, to escape from the kāma-loka, because there is nothing pleasant in this outside world of five senses. So, they turn away inwardly.

  2. 5 Ven. Ñāṇavimala advised Shanaka to seek employment in fields like the Red Cross, civil service, or as a carer/attendant. The emphasis was on choosing a livelihood
    (    ājīva) that aligned with the Dhamma (Right Livelihood) and left ample time for practice, rather than just pursuing large monetary gain. Such work generates wholesome kamma.

  3. 6 Mahānāma was a prominent Sakyan layman and a relative of the Buddha, known for asking practical questions about how a layperson can cultivate faith and virtue. The practices often discussed in these discourses include the recollections (anussati) of the Buddha (Buddhānussati), the Dhamma (Dhammānussati), and the Saṅgha (Saṅghānussati), as well as the cultivation of loving-kindness (mettā) and the perception of impermanence (anicca saññā). These teachings can be found in several discourses addressed to him: AN6.10; AN8.25; AN11.11; AN11.12; SN55.21; SN55.22; SN55.37.

Nyanatiloka also included brief explanatory passages and summaries drawn from the Abhidhamma and the commentarial understanding. Ven.
    Ñāṇavimala encouraged Shanaka to focus just on the presentations of the early texts themselves.

  8. 11 These teachings are found in several discourses given to Mahānāma, the Sakyan prince and cousin of the Buddha, including: AN6.10; AN8.25; AN11.11; AN11.12; SN55.21; SN55.22; SN55.37.

    meditators as they lay a foundational understanding of the core principles of the Dhamma that lead directly to the path of liberation.
