Chapter 09

Give a Finger to Māra,
He Takes a Hand
(Jan 2000 – May 2000)

[Shanaka Dharmakeerthi was ordained as Venerable Bambalapitiye Gnanaloka on 19 December 1999; these notes were written after his ordination.]

11 January 2000

x Make sure the main practice is well established,
concentrating on the necessary task. Later when the need or the necessity is felt you may add the required knowledge for that stage. (Avoid being a scholar or a meditator without paññā).

x Establish in mettā bhāvanā as told in Aṅguttara—Mettā Sutta (AN8.1; loving-kindness) and then Khandhasaṁyutta and Saḷāyatanasaṁyutta—Khandha Saṁyutta (SN22; connected discourses on the aggregates); Saḷāyatana Saṁyutta (SN35; connected discourses on the six sense bases)

x Slowly start in the nearby area piṇḍapāta (alms round/gathering of alms food) and slowly extend to further areas.

x Know the branches of Galduwa and Wathuruwila [forest monasteries], so you could easily find your way to those places when going on cārikā.

x When you make the determination to live according to the teachings of the Buddha, that is kamma. Going on cārikā is very hard, you will fall sick. But then only there is experience of dukkha (suffering) in kāmaloka (the sensual realm).

x Otherwise if you get used to the place and all its comforts, being looked after by the dāyakas. It is dangerous. Think of the first 60 arahants, how they died and where they passed away.12

x First you will have to go with the teacher. Progress in the Dhamma is not at once, you must allow time.

x When in the jungle, don’t get frightened or excited when faced with animals because they perceive the state of mind. Recite the Khandha Paritta—[Ahirāja Sutta (AN4.67; snakes)] daily and act with mettā to these beings.

x If the monk you are under (the teacher's senior pupil) does not have an understanding of the actual practice, then you might have to part from him. This must not be done at once. When such things come, you must call for a Saṅgha meeting. Because it is not by chance you get this and that one as your upajjhāya (preceptor) or ācariya (teacher). There is a certain kamma behind it.

23 January 2000

x When you are striving and living according to the Dhamma, merit is made and devas in the higher world will give protection as they like a person who can give merit. They will follow, don’t forget that.

x Though things have changed outside, if you are determined to live according to the Dhamma, that will not be in vain.

x Though things have changed in Ceylon from outside, still in the hearts of the people the true idea of the monks is not lost.13

4 May 2000

x When you give a finger to Māra, he takes a hand. So be careful of this kāmasukha (sensual pleasure). If some bhikkhus continue in this way, over the course of time, as people look for a leader, [those bhikkhus would be the ones] they would follow.14

x “Ekāsanaṁ ekaseyyaṁ, eko caraṁ atandito;
eko damayaṁ attānaṁ, vanante ramito siyā”

“Sitting alone, sleeping alone, tirelessly wandering alone; one who tames themselves alone would delight within a forest.”

(Dhp 305)

  1. 12 “The first 60 arahants” refers to the Buddha’s initial enlightened disciples, whom he immediately sent out to teach. The Buddha was leaving the comfort and care provided by his attending supporters (dāyakas)—the food, medicine, robes and dwellings they offered—to prioritize his mission. The statement challenges the monk to reject the ease of dependence on the laity in favor of the duty to teach the Dhamma.

  2. 13 Ven. Ñāṇavimala often emphasized that while social conditions may shift, the fundamental faith of the Sri Lankan lay community toward sincere monks remains strong, rooted in centuries of devotion to the Saṅgha.

  3. 14 Ven. Ñāṇavimala’s primary warning is against the danger of sensual pleasure (kāmasukha) corrupting the spiritual path. However, Venerable Gnanaloka adds that when spiritual practitioners maintain this disciplined, virtuous spiritual life, they naturally become figures who inspire and lead others to follow the Dhamma.
