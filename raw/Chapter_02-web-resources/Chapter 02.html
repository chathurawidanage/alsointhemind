Chapter 02

The Buddha Never Lets You Down
(May 1994 – June 1994)

22 May 1994

x The phenomena that you encounter, look at them as things conditioned and subject to change.

x You may practice mettā bhāvanā.

x When you practice Satipaṭṭhāna, greed, hate and delusion slowly reduces.

x Read the verses in the Dhammapada (Dhp; verses of the Dhamma), and memorise some.

x Advice to Rāhula—Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda Sutta (MN61; advice to Rāhula at Ambalaṭṭhikā). Practice the three ways of conduct, so that there would be a sound basis, you may reflect on your actions every evening, to see if there was any transgression, and determine to avoid it in the future.

x Girimānanda Sutta (AN10.60; Girimānanda).

x Mahāparinibbāna Sutta (DN16; the great passing, the Buddha’s last days).

x Word of the Buddha [by Venerable Nyanatiloka Mahāthera].

x Never let the mind be in a state of worry or fear. Always, it should be a pleasant abiding.

x When we are practicing, though we may be far away in body, we will be united in mind.

x Recommended Aranyas [forest monasteries]: Galduwa, Wathuruwila, Thapowanaya (Angoda) and Kalugala.15

x If you become a monk, you may not get the chance in the beginning; to practice the way you did when you were here (2.5 months). You might have to get involved in activities like learning Pāli and so forth. After five years you may spend more time on the practice.

x May you be of service to your relatives and later to all beings.

21 June 1994

x You mustn’t plan anything, if you do you might face disappointment. But you must try to come close to Dhamma in that given environment.

x You must realise saṁsāra dukkha [the suffering inherent in the cycle of repeated existence].

x There is attachment and aversion due to ignorance.

x Everything in the world is covered by a thin surface. If it is penetrated there would not arise attachment.

x Sometimes there is a danger that some people get used to repulsive perceptions, and it changes to a pleasurable activity. e.g. Doctors who dissect, people who catch fish—they get used to doing evil.

x Remember these examples: Seeing the open ulcer in the body, he became a hermit in Egypt.16
The other one who was too tall, so then her head was chopped off to put the body in the coffin. Seeing the head.17

x One day reverend sir [Ven. Ñāṇavimala] visited a dissecting theatre of human beings, a leg here, some flesh there and so on.

x The Buddha never lets you down.

x Do something [an occupation] that would give enough time [to practice] and would be in accordance with Dhamma.

x Don’t think of a particular teacher and so on.

x All the time there would be obstacles. You must work for it as a layman. Then things would change if you do things genuinely.

x There should be a good basis in meritorious actions, and the three ways of conduct are very important for a good foundation.

x When you make a determination, that is kamma; it will bear fruit in the future.

x You are facing this situation (studies) because you have given support previously, and now you are getting it. Something you liked and valued previously.18

x When your father supports you in this way, he will get similar support in the future. If he supports you spiritually, he will get the same in the future.

x Don’t take any steps without your father’s approval.

x All the people in Sri Lanka were in India before.19

x What is inside will manifest to outside.20

  1. 15 Ven. Ñāṇavimala recommended several prominent Sri Lankan monasteries and their branches, including Galduwa Aranya Senasanaya, Ambalangoda; Wathuruwila Aranya Senasanaya in Galle; Gothama Thapowanaya in Angoda, Colombo; and Kalugala Aranya Senasanaya in Kalutara. Detailed information can be found in Buddhist Forest Monasteries and Meditation Centres in Sri Lanka: A Guide for Foreign Buddhist Monastics and Lay Practitioners by Bhikkhu Nyanatusita.

  2. 16 Ven. Gnanaloka suggested the likely source: This refers to the German philosopher, Arthur Schopenhauer, who told of a man who, upon seeing a naked woman, felt desire, but after noticing an open ulcer on her body, was so struck by the reality of decay that he abandoned worldly life to live as a hermit in Egypt.

  3. 17 Ven. Gnanaloka was unsure of the source. The anecdote bears resemblance to two distinct historical accounts: that of Queen Anne Boleyn (executed 1536), whose head and body were reportedly placed in a makeshift chest; and that of Empress Isabella of Portugal (d. 1539), whose disfigured corpse is said to have prompted Duke Francis Borgia to renounce worldly life.

  4. 18 Ven. Gnanaloka clarifies: he has liked studies in a previous life, and supported
    someone to study in that life, perhaps even forcing it onto somebody. This
    consequence of     kamma is coming back to him in this life with his father pressuring him to focus on his studies. He also adds that if his father supports him in the
    spiritual way, he will get similar support in the future.

  5. 19 Ven. Gnanaloka clarifies that Ven. Ñāṇavimala speaks about the similarities of Sri Lankan and Indian people, as they share the same roots.

  6. 20 Ven. Gnanaloka clarifies that this has multiple layers of meaning—what you are experiencing is related to your internal world such as defilements and so on. A further meaning is that if you have a strong intention within you, it may create those conditions outside. A third meaning is that seeing the faults of others outside is a reflection of what is inside you.
