# This Is Also in the Mind

### *In the Presence of Venerable Ñāṇavimala Mahāthera*

> *"This is also in the mind."*  
> — **Venerable Ñāṇavimala Mahāthera**, to his young disciple on a roadside in Panadura, cutting through layers of intellectual realization to reveal the nature of the mind.

---

## Overview

**This Is Also in the Mind** is an intimate account of a disciple's spiritual training under Venerable Ñāṇavimala Mahāthera (1911–2005)—a German-born Buddhist monk who walked deeper into the Buddha's path than most dare to go, living with uncompromising simplicity, solitude, and purity in Sri Lankan forests.

Preserved in private for a quarter of a century, these handwritten training notes and personal encounters capture a direct, heart-to-heart transmission of the Early Buddhist teachings rarely documented in the modern era.

The book is published and freely distributed as *Dhammadāna* (gift of Dhamma) by the **Sri Sambuddha Mission Charitable Trust**.

---

## The Master and the Disciple

### The Master: Venerable Ñāṇavimala Mahāthera
Born Friedrich Möller in Germany in 1911. A job posting in Asia offered him an escape from Nazi conscription; upon the outbreak of World War II, he was interned in Ceylon as an "enemy alien." In that internment camp, he encountered the Buddha's words through Venerable Ñāṇatiloka Mahāthera.

Ordained in 1955 at the Polgasduwa Island Hermitage, he spent eleven years in training before undertaking twenty-five years as a solitary wandering ascetic (*cārikā*). Walking the length and breadth of Sri Lanka barefoot with only his robes and an alms bowl, he shunned institutions, followers, and possessions, embodying radical renunciation and unwavering adherence to the Buddha's original discourses (*Suttas*).

### The Disciple: Venerable Bambalapitiye Gnanaloka Thero
In 1990, an eighteen-year-old student named Shanaka Dharmakeerthi crossed paths with an elderly foreign monk on a roadside in Colombo. That encounter ignited a sixteen-year discipleship. Between 1994 and 2001, through solitary retreats and brief encounters, he meticulously recorded his master's direct instructions in notebooks.

Carried through Himalayan journeys and deep Sri Lankan hermitages, these notes were carefully preserved for twenty-five years before being prepared for publication so that future practitioners could benefit from this living lineage.

## Book Architecture

The book is structured in alternating layers, designed to serve both beginning seekers and serious meditators:

### Part I: The Master's Instructions (Notes)
Original, unfiltered records from 1994–2001. Delivered in concise, direct, and urgent language, these notes form a rigorous training curriculum directly rooted in the Pāli *Suttas*. They provide clear instructions on:
- Sense restraint (*indriya-saṁvara*)
- Overcoming sensual craving and mental attachments
- Deepening tranquility and insight meditation
- Anchoring one's practice strictly in the Buddha's words

### Part II: The Living Witness (Reflections)
Thematic narrative accounts crafted from Ven. Gnanaloka's recollections and public teachings. These contemplative chapters provide context for the notes, portraying what dedicated practice looks like in flesh and blood:
- Unshakable composure when confronted by armed robbers
- Radiating *mettā* (loving-kindness) in the wilderness
- Meeting physical deterioration and old age with radical equanimity
- Walking fearlessly with only the Dhamma as protection

## Contents at a Glance

### Preliminaries & Memoirs
- **Foreword** — Reflections by Venerable Bambalapitiye Gnanaloka Thero on impermanence, preserving the *Suttas*, and fulfilling his duty to his teacher.
- **By the Roadside, 1990** — The fateful roadside encounter in Colombo that altered the course of a lifetime.
- **Before It Was Too Late** — The disciple's vow, the hundred-fold promise, and preserving the master's legacy.
- **From Friedrich Möller to Venerable Ñāṇavimala Mahāthera** — The biographical journey from pre-war Germany to forest asceticism in Ceylon.
- **A Reader's Guide** — Practical guidance on how to navigate the Notes and Reflections according to your level of practice.

### Chapters
1. **Avoid Every Bondage With The World** — Notes from Island Hermitage (1994) & Living as an Island Unto Oneself.
2. **The Buddha Never Lets You Down** — Anchoring total trust in the Triple Gem.
3. **The Way You Deal With Your Parents** — Fulfilling filial duties without compromising spiritual renunciation.
4. **More Pleasure More Danger** — Seeing through sensual traps and the illusion of worldly satisfaction.
5. **Always Be With Thoughts Of Mettā** — Developing boundless loving-kindness as an impenetrable shield.
6. **Restrain, Abstain And Endure** — The triad of monastic fortitude: enduring hardship and guarding the senses.
7. **Stick To The Exact Words Of The Buddha** — Returning directly to the Nikāyas rather than later interpretations.
8. **What Doesn’t Arise, Doesn’t Pass Away** — Contemplations on cessation, consciousness, and the unconditioned.
9. **Give A Finger To Māra, He Takes A Hand** — Vigilance against subtle spiritual compromises.
10. **Find A Way To Bring Many To This Path** — Cultivating compassion and opening the gateway of liberation for others.

### Practical Appendices
- **Guidance for Lay Practitioners** — Teachings on ethics, generosity, and meditation in household life.
- **Guidance for Monastics** — Comprehensive training advice for those who have gone forth.
- **Walking Contemplations (*Caṅkama*)** — The precise contemplations Venerable Ñāṇavimala practiced during walking meditation.
- **Canonical References** — A complete concordance of all *Suttas* referenced throughout the text.

## Core Themes

- **Return to the Suttas**: An insistence on going directly to the Buddha's discourses (*Suttas*) as living instructions rather than scholarly theory.
- **Radical Simplicity**: A reminder of the supreme freedom found in owning almost nothing and craving nothing from the world.
- **Living as an Island (*Atta-dīpa*)**: Cultivating internal refuge and self-reliance while staying steadfast on the path to liberation.
- **Visible Here and Now (*Sandiṭṭhiko*)**: Dhamma to be realized individually by the wise through direct personal investigation (*ehipassiko*).
